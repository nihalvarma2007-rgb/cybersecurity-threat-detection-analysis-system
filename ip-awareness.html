An adversary here isn't "notified" of anything — they're simply positioned somewhere your traffic physically passes: a shared public Wi-Fi network, a compromised or malicious router, or anyone with visibility into ISP-level traffic. They watch packets as they pass, in both directions, in real time.

A server (or anyone logging its traffic) only ever sees your public IP — never your private/local one. Geolocation from IP is city-level at best, often wrong, and never reveals a precise address, name, or device.

Lookups use ipify.org (your public IP) and ipapi.co (approximate IP/host geolocation) — free public APIs, called directly from your browser. World borders are real, from the public-domain Natural Earth dataset (via world-atlas), rendered client-side with D3.
For a production project, proxy these calls through your own backend to hide rate limits and add caching. Approximate location data should never be treated as precise or used for access control.
